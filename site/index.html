01 — the source

It starts with one ordinary photo.

No scanner, no turntable, no modelling software. A single picture of a cat, sitting still enough for one frame. That is the entire input.

The point of the exercise is that the barrier is gone. A student who has never opened a 3D program can hold a physical object that started as something they photographed.

02 — photo to mesh

AI guesses the other side of the cat.

An image-to-3D model takes the single photograph and infers the whole shape — the back, the far flank, the parts the camera never saw — then paints a texture over it. What comes back is a real, printable mesh in about a minute.

03 — two hours later

A whole tray, in the time it takes to print one.

An LCD resin printer cures an entire layer in a single flash of UV light. Filling the plate with cats costs nothing extra — twenty of them take the same two hours as one, because every layer is exposed all at once.

That is the idea the whole workshop is built around, and it is the thing students find hardest to believe until they watch it happen.
